📖 Manager's Manual

1. Weather & Recipes

Different weather requires different recipes. Observe the daily data tables to see how Taste reacts to Amount. You can save a successful recipe for a specific weather type using the checkbox above the Brew button.

2. Economics & Pricing

Your Margin is Price minus Unit Cost. You pay $150 Rent daily. For the first 4 days, a friendly donor will cover your losses!

3. Marketing & Hot Streaks (Buzz)

If you achieve a Taste Score of 75 or higher AND make a positive profit, you trigger a Hot Streak! Each day on a Hot Streak adds +5 permanent daily customers to your shop. Miss the mark, and the streak resets to zero.

4. The Coffee Snob Blog

If you manage to brew a nearly perfect recipe (Taste Score 85+) for 3 consecutive Rainy Days, a coffee snob will blog about it for a massive surge of customers! But beware: brewing a terrible recipe (<40 Taste) on a Rainy day will trigger a bad review, hurting your sales the next day!

5. Corporate Espionage & Suspicion

Leak fake recipes to Roaster Co. Every time you successfully trick them, they get smarter! They will begin to demand a higher Taste Score (Suspicion Threshold) before they believe your leak. Solve the quadratic inequality (y > threshold) to find an x-value that still causes financial damage!

📐 The Math Guide

Taste Score follows a quadratic curve. Finding the peak (Vertex) maximizes profit.

Tier 1: Observations (Secant Lines)

Over the first 3 days, points are revealed. If the Taste Score goes UP as Amount goes UP, you have a positive slope and haven't hit the peak yet!

Tier 2: Better Points

You get two points on a new table that have the exact same Taste Score. Because parabolas are symmetrical, the perfect amount is exactly in the middle. Use (x₁ + x₂) / 2.

Tier 3: Standard Form

You are given P(x) = ax² + bx + c. Use the vertex formula:
x = -b / (2a)

Tier 4: Vertex Form

You are given P(x) = a(x - h)² + k.
h is the "Perfect Amount" for your slider.

Sabotage (Quadratic Inequalities)

To sabotage successfully, you must provide an x-value that yields a Taste Score (y) greater than their Suspicion Threshold. Calculate an x where a(x - h)² + k > Suspicion.
